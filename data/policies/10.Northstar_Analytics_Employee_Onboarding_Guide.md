# Northstar Analytics Employee Onboarding Guide

*A coordinated path from accepted offer through the first 90 days*

| **Guide owner** | People Operations | **Version** | 1.0 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Effective date** | September 27 2026 | **Applies to** | New hires and onboarding participants |

## 1 Purpose and Use

This guide coordinates the activities that help a new employee become equipped, connected, and productive during the first 90 days. It identifies the sequence, owners, checkpoints, and completion evidence for onboarding. It does not restate the detailed rules in Northstar policies, benefit materials, security standards, or role procedures; those documents remain controlling.

People Operations starts the companywide workflow. The manager adds a role-specific plan based on the employee's position, experience, work location, and client responsibilities. Rehires, transfers, and employees returning after a long absence may use an adapted plan approved by People Operations and the manager.

## 2 Onboarding Roles

- **New employee.** Completes assigned actions, asks questions, attends scheduled sessions, and flags access or workload barriers promptly.
- **Manager.** Defines early outcomes, assigns meaningful work, schedules check-ins, introduces key partners, and confirms readiness.
- **People Operations.** Coordinates employment records, required notices, policy acknowledgments, orientation, and completion tracking.
- **Information Technology and Security.** Provision approved equipment and access, deliver required setup guidance, and resolve access risks.
- **Onboarding partner.** Provides practical team context and navigation support without replacing the manager or policy owner.

## 3 Before the Start Date

| Owner | Required outcome | Completion evidence |
|---|---|---|
| People Operations | Employment details, start instructions, required forms, and orientation schedule are issued. | Completed record or confirmed appointment |
| Manager | A 30 60 90 day role plan, initial calendar, and first assignment are prepared. | Plan shared with employee |
| IT and Security | Approved equipment, accounts, and role-based access are staged for the start date. | Provisioning ticket or access record |
| New employee | Requested prehire information is returned through the approved method. | Submission confirmation |

## 4 First Day

The first day establishes orientation and working context. The employee confirms that essential access works, meets the manager and immediate team, reviews the week's schedule, and learns where to find current policies and support channels. The manager explains the role's purpose, current priorities, decision boundaries, and what a successful first week looks like. Any blocked access, missing equipment, or accessibility need is routed to the responsible owner the same day.

## 5 First Week

The first week should build safe independence without overwhelming the employee. Required learning and acknowledgments are assigned through the approved system. The manager sequences introductions and work so each activity supports an immediate responsibility rather than serving as a general information tour.

- **Work context.** Review team objectives, active commitments, decision paths, recurring meetings, and the systems used to document work.
- **Policy orientation.** Locate and acknowledge the Employee Handbook and assigned policies. Direct detailed questions to the document owner rather than paraphrasing requirements.
- **Role learning.** Review the role profile, quality expectations, key workflows, and examples of acceptable deliverables.
- **Relationship map.** Meet the people needed for daily work, approvals, client delivery, technical support, and employee support.
- **Initial contribution.** Complete a bounded task that allows the manager to observe communication, judgment, and support needs.

## 6 Days 8 Through 30

During the remainder of the first month, the employee moves from orientation to supervised contribution. The manager narrows priorities to a manageable set of outcomes and provides access to the instructions, examples, data, and reviewers needed to complete them. Training should be assigned when it supports current work or satisfies a documented requirement.

| Focus | Manager action | Employee action |
|---|---|---|
| Goals | Confirm first-month deliverables and measures of completion. | Restate priorities and raise unclear or conflicting expectations. |
| Work practice | Observe at least one representative task and provide specific feedback. | Apply feedback and document decisions or open questions. |
| Connections | Introduce cross-functional partners needed for active work. | Confirm how and when to engage each partner. |
| Enablement | Review access and learning gaps; route unresolved items. | Report blockers before they affect a commitment. |

## 7 Thirty Day Checkpoint

The manager and employee hold a documented checkpoint near day 30. They review completed work, feedback received, role clarity, working relationships, required learning, and unresolved barriers. They then update the next 60 days of the plan. This checkpoint is developmental and does not replace any formal performance review, accommodation process, leave process, or workplace concern procedure.

## 8 Practical Escalation Path

- **Access equipment or technical issue.** Use the approved support channel and include the business impact and needed timing.
- **Role priority or workload issue.** Raise it with the manager before a commitment is missed.
- **Employment benefit leave or accommodation question.** Contact People Operations for the governing process and confidential handling.
- **Security or data concern.** Use the security reporting channel immediately; do not wait for a routine check-in.
- **Conduct harassment or retaliation concern.** Use any reporting channel listed in the Employee Handbook or applicable policy.

## 9 Days 31 Through 60

The second month emphasizes consistent execution. The employee takes ownership of recurring responsibilities or a defined workstream while the manager calibrates independence to demonstrated readiness. Check-ins address results, quality, collaboration, judgment, and emerging skill needs. The onboarding plan should be updated when business priorities change; changes should be explicit rather than left for the employee to infer.

## 10 Days 61 Through 90

The final onboarding phase confirms sustainable performance expectations and ongoing development. The employee should be able to identify normal responsibilities, escalation paths, required records, and the people or resources needed to complete work. The manager provides a clear summary of progress, remaining gaps, and priorities after onboarding.

## 11 Ninety Day Completion Review

| Review area | Completion question |
|---|---|
| Role clarity | Can the employee explain core responsibilities, current priorities, and decision boundaries? |
| Work readiness | Are essential tools, access, workflows, and role instructions available and usable? |
| Required actions | Are assigned forms, acknowledgments, and required learning complete or formally rescheduled? |
| Relationships | Does the employee know the manager, key partners, support owners, and reporting channels? |
| Performance direction | Are near-term goals, feedback, development actions, and check-in cadence documented? |

The manager records the completion review in the approved location and sends any required confirmation to People Operations. Open items remain assigned to a named owner with a target date. Completion of onboarding confirms that the planned process occurred; it is not a guarantee of employment, a waiver of performance expectations, or a substitute for another required review.

## 12 Records Exceptions and Adjustments

Onboarding records should show assigned actions, completion status, checkpoint notes, and unresolved items without including unnecessary medical, investigative, or other sensitive details. Confidential records belong only in the approved restricted system. People Operations determines the retention location for companywide onboarding records; managers maintain role plans in the approved performance or work system.

People Operations may adjust timing or sequence for delayed equipment, protected leave, an accommodation, a revised start date, urgent business conditions, or another documented need. A manager may adapt role learning but may not waive mandatory employment, security, legal, or client requirements. The responsible policy owner must approve any permitted exception to a governing requirement.
